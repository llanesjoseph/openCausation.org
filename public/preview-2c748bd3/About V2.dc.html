ABT-01
About

Purpose, Scope, and Foundation

OpenCausation.org™ is an independent, open, noncommercial educational resource dedicated to improving the clarity, consistency, and evidence basis of causation analysis. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or a product of the American Medical Association.

The site provides general educational information only. It does not provide medical, legal, or case-specific causation advice. Case-specific causation determinations require an objective diagnosis, complete records, clinical judgment, exposure analysis, relevant scientific evidence, consideration of alternative explanations, and application of the legal standards of the appropriate jurisdiction. Worked examples are hypothetical and illustrative only.

This resource is grounded in established causation-analysis principles, including the framework set out in the AMA Guides to the Evaluation of Disease and Injury Causation, 2nd ed. (2014), occupational and epidemiologic methods, and related causation-analysis materials developed by J. Mark Melhorn, MD.

ABT-02
Mission

To make causation analysis more transparent, consistent, and scientifically defensible — by providing open educational access to structured methods, core concepts, worked examples, and practical tools for evidence-based decision-making.

OpenCausation.org™ is designed for everyone who relies on the causation answer — including clinicians, IME providers, attorneys, insurers, employers, adjudicators, policymakers, and individuals whose lives may be affected by the conclusion.

ABT-03
Core commitments
CC-01Integrity and neutralityApply the same method regardless of who requests the analysis or which party the conclusion may favor.
CC-02Scientific rigorGround conclusions in credible evidence, sound methodology, and the limits of what the evidence can support.
CC-03TransparencyIdentify assumptions, uncertainties, limitations, and contrary evidence rather than converting probabilistic science into false certainty.
CC-04Education and accessProvide clear, practical, non-commercial resources that help professionals and decision-makers understand and apply evidence-based causation methods.
CC-05Continuous improvementEncourage refinement of causation science through research, quality improvement, interdisciplinary collaboration, and responsible use of emerging knowledge tools.
ABT-04
In short

OpenCausation.org™ was created to make causation analysis more transparent, consistent, and evidence-based — a method, not a verdict.
